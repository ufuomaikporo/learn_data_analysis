# How to interpret salary figures

Career stages used throughout: early career = 0–3 years of relevant experience; developing = 3–5 years; experienced = 5–10 years; leadership = typically 10+ years. These are planning definitions, not standardized employer categories. Prior finance experience can count toward some roles even if analytics experience is new.

IMPORTANT: Reliable Canadian datasets generally report an occupation-wide median, not separate medians for 0–3 and 5–10 years for each niche title. Therefore, the stage-specific figures below are indicative midpoint estimates (rounded) based on published salary bands and related role benchmarks; they are NOT observed statistical medians. Use them for comparison, not as guaranteed offers. Bonuses, commissions, profit-sharing and equity are excluded.

# 2 \| Finance Data Analyst: General Skills Roadmap

Goal: qualify for entry-level Financial Data Analyst, Banking Data Analyst, Finance BI Analyst, Revenue Analyst, or financial reporting analyst positions across banking, insurance, fintech, investment services, and corporate finance. Designed for someone with finance knowledge but limited analytics experience; study 8–10 hours weekly for roughly 7–9 months.

## Phase 1 \| Excel and financial data fundamentals (Weeks 1–4)

- Learn tables, PivotTables, XLOOKUP, SUMIFS, charts, Power Query, data cleaning, and basic financial KPIs (revenue, margin, variance, cash flow, delinquency).

- Deliverable: budget-versus-actual workbook with clear variance commentary and a one-page management summary.

## Phase 2 \| SQL and relational databases (Weeks 5–10)

- Learn SELECT, filtering, JOINs, GROUP BY, HAVING, CASE, subqueries, CTEs, window functions, dates, NULL handling, and data quality checks.

- Deliverable: query a mock banking or sales database to report monthly transaction volume, revenue, customer segments, and exceptions; save annotated SQL scripts.

## Phase 3 \| Power BI and reporting (Weeks 11–16)

- Learn Power Query transformations, star-schema data modeling, relationships, measures vs. calculated columns, core DAX, filters, drilldowns, and dashboard design.

- Deliverable: interactive financial performance dashboard with documented KPIs, trend analysis, and a short business recommendation.

## Phase 4 \| Statistics and analytical thinking (Weeks 17–20)

- Learn distributions, averages vs. medians, variance, percent changes, outliers, sampling, correlation vs. causation, confidence intervals, and introductory regression.

- Deliverable: investigate a business question such as declining profitability or rising late payments, documenting assumptions and limitations.

## Phase 5 \| Python for analysts (Weeks 21–26)

- Learn Python basics, Jupyter, pandas, CSV/Excel import, merging, grouping, missing values, plotting, and repeatable reporting; use Git/GitHub for version control.

- Deliverable: a Python notebook that cleans transaction data, checks quality, calculates KPIs, and exports a reproducible monthly report.

## Phase 6 \| End-to-end portfolio and job readiness (Weeks 27–36)

- Complete two polished projects: (1) SQL + Power BI banking/finance dashboard; (2) Excel + Python financial performance or customer/credit trend analysis.

- For each project include a README, data dictionary, assumptions, SQL/notebook files, dashboard screenshots, key findings, and a concise business recommendation. Use synthetic or public data, not confidential customer information.

- Practice SQL joins/window functions, Excel modeling, Power BI measures, data-quality scenarios, stakeholder storytelling, and common finance KPIs. Tailor resume to Financial Data Analyst, Banking Data Analyst, Finance BI Analyst, and Reporting Analyst listings.

## Job-ready checklist

- Can independently clean data in Excel/Power Query; write intermediate SQL; build and explain a Power BI dashboard; use pandas for basic analysis; communicate a business finding; and demonstrate two reproducible projects.

## Optional specialization after the foundation

- FP&A: forecasting and three-statement modeling. Risk: credit metrics and regression. Investments: valuation and portfolio performance. Quant: advanced mathematics and time series. These are extensions, not prerequisites for general finance data analyst jobs.

# 3 \| Finance Analytics Career Profiles

## 1. Quantitative Finance

**Common titles:** Quantitative Analyst; Quant Researcher; Model Validation Analyst; Quantitative Risk Analyst

**Where they work:** Investment banks, hedge funds, proprietary trading, pension funds, insurers

**Day-to-day work:** Design mathematical models for pricing, trading, portfolio optimization or risk; validate assumptions; code and backtest models.

**Core skills:** Advanced probability, calculus, linear algebra, statistics, Python; SQL; time series; financial derivatives; C++ for some roles.

**Credentials / education:** Mathematics/statistics/computer science graduate study is common in research; CQF may help certain candidates but is not a substitute for fundamentals.

**Career reality:** Research quant roles often require advanced degrees and exceptionally strong mathematics; consider quantitative risk as an adjacent entry point.

## 2. Investment / Portfolio Analytics

**Common titles:** Investment Analyst; Portfolio Analyst; Performance Analyst; Equity Research Analyst

**Where they work:** Asset management, pension funds, wealth management, investment banking

**Day-to-day work:** Evaluate securities and issuers, construct valuations, attribute portfolio performance, communicate investment recommendations.

**Core skills:** Financial statements, valuation, Excel, Python, SQL, statistics, portfolio metrics, investment writing.

**Credentials / education:** CFA program is widely relevant for investment analysis; check actual role requirements.

**Career reality:** Investment research can be competitive; performance reporting or portfolio operations can be a more accessible first role.

## 3. Risk / Credit Analytics

**Common titles:** Credit Risk Analyst; Risk Data Analyst; Model Risk Analyst; Fraud Analytics Analyst

**Where they work:** Commercial banks, credit unions, insurers, consumer lenders, fintech

**Day-to-day work:** Measure probability of default, portfolio concentration, exposure and loss; monitor risk limits; validate models and reporting.

**Core skills:** SQL, Python/pandas, probability, regression, classification, Power BI, financial ratios, model governance.

**Credentials / education:** FRM can be useful in financial risk; advanced modeling roles may expect graduate-level statistics.

**Career reality:** Good balance of technical depth and finance domain advantage; real risk models also require regulatory controls and independent validation.

## 4. FP&A / Financial Analytics

**Common titles:** Financial Analyst; FP&A Analyst; Senior Financial Analyst; FP&A Manager

**Where they work:** Corporate finance in energy, retail, manufacturing, healthcare, technology and financial services

**Day-to-day work:** Budget, forecast, investigate variances, model profitability and support executive decisions.

**Core skills:** Excel, financial statements, Power Query, Power BI, SQL, scenario analysis, business partnering; Python is useful but not always essential.

**Credentials / education:** CPA is valuable for accounting-heavy leadership paths; FPAC is another optional credential.

**Career reality:** Often the fastest transition for a person with prior corporate finance or accounting experience.

## 5. Finance BI / Data Analytics

**Common titles:** Financial Data Analyst; Finance BI Analyst; Banking Data Analyst; Revenue Analyst

**Where they work:** Banks, insurance, fintech, payment processors, corporate finance

**Day-to-day work:** Integrate financial data, define KPIs, build reliable reports and dashboards, and investigate business trends.

**Core skills:** SQL, data modeling, Power BI, DAX, Excel, Python/pandas, data quality, stakeholder communication.

**Credentials / education:** Microsoft PL-300 can demonstrate Power BI skills; cloud certificates can wait.

**Career reality:** Broadest cross-industry mobility; especially suitable for candidates who enjoy technology more than valuation.

# 4 \| Shared foundation and decision guide

Complete the general Finance Data Analyst roadmap first. Then select a specialization based on interests, existing finance experience, and target job postings. The career profiles below describe optional advanced paths, not separate prerequisites for entry-level analyst work.

- Maximum specialist compensation potential: Quantitative Finance, but the steepest mathematics and hiring barriers.

- Best blend of finance and technical modeling: Risk / Credit Analytics.

- Most direct use of accounting/budgeting background: FP&A.

- Most portable technical skills: Finance BI / Data Analytics.

- Best fit for markets and company valuation: Investment / Portfolio Analytics.

# 5 \| Suggested application milestones

- By month 2: finish one Excel or SQL mini-project and review 20 target job descriptions.

- By month 4–5: publish one documented portfolio project with business questions and methodology.

- By month 6–8: complete a second project in the chosen specialization and begin targeted applications when baseline requirements are met.

- By month 9–12: strengthen interview cases, domain knowledge and a third project if needed.

- For quant research: expect a longer, more academic preparation path than the other tracks.
